# Prompt 03 — Stakeholder Translation

## Business context

The most common communication failure in AI initiatives is not dishonesty — it is a mismatch between how AI practitioners talk and how decision-makers, regulators, and affected communities think.

An AI leader who can translate technical AI content for any audience is more valuable than one who can only communicate with peers. This prompt operationalises that skill.

Designed for:
- Policy and communications roles at AI companies
- AI strategy leads presenting to boards or government stakeholders
- Responsible AI practitioners explaining system decisions to affected users
- Partnerships leads preparing client-facing materials from technical documentation

**Why a prompt approach works here:** Audience-adapted rewriting is a reliable LLM task. The model is good at register-shifting and explanation. The human must supply the judgment about what matters for the specific audience — which this prompt structures explicitly.

## Failure modes

**1. Underdescribed audience produces generic output.**
"Non-technical stakeholder" is not enough. The more specific the audience description and their actual concerns, the more useful the translation.

**2. The model may over-simplify to the point of inaccuracy.**
Always review the output against the original to check no important caveat has been erased.

**3. Tone calibration varies.**
For community or citizen audiences, the output may need a second pass to feel genuinely human rather than institutional.

---

## When not to use this

- Do not use to make uncertain AI capabilities sound more reliable than they are. This prompt is for translation, not spin.
- Do not use for legal or regulatory communications without qualified review.
- Do not use to translate content you do not yourself understand. You cannot catch the model's errors if you are not familiar with the source.
